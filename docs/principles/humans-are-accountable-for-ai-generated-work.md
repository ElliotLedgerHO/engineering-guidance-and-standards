A named person or team is accountable for all work produced with artificial intelligence (AI). A tool, model or agent is never accountable, whatever share of the work it did.

If you use AI to write code, make a decision or produce an output, you own the result as if you had produced it without AI.

---

## Rationale

The Home Office must be able to explain and stand behind its services and decisions. A tool, model or agent cannot take on that responsibility. Only a person or team can answer for a decision, fix a problem or own an outcome.

AI tools quickly produce large amounts of well-presented output. Polished output can still be wrong, insecure or unfair. A named owner must therefore answer for checking the work and fixing problems.

Accountability also helps teams use AI where it adds value. A named owner who understands the outcome and the cost is more likely to ask whether AI is the right tool for the task.

---

## Applications and Implications

To apply this principle, you should:

- hold AI-generated work to the same standards and quality bar as any other work, as set out in [Hold AI-generated work to existing standards](/standards/hold-ai-generated-work-to-existing-standards/)
- record a named owner for every AI system you use or build, as set out in [Name an owner for every AI system](/standards/name-an-owner-for-every-ai-system/)
- tell the people affected by a decision or output what role AI played in producing it, for example in a pull request description or in service documentation
- use AI only where the use is fair, avoids harm and gives a real benefit compared with its effort and cost
